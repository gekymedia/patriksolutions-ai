# Module 4 — Customer Service With AI

**Estimated time:** ~75 minutes · **Lessons:** 4

## Learning objectives
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Draft fast, consistent, on-brand replies to common customer messages.
- Handle complaints and upset customers with calm, effective responses.
- Build a reusable "reply library" for your most common questions.
- Keep customer data private and know where a human must step in.

> This module leans hardest on Course 1's privacy rules — customer messages often contain personal data. We'll build the speed *and* keep it safe.

---

## Lesson 4.1 — The reply pattern for common questions

Most customer messages are variations of the same few questions: hours, availability, pricing, "where's my order," "can you help with X." AI turns these from a drain into seconds.

**The customer-reply prompt:**
```
Act as a friendly customer-service rep for my [business]. 
A customer asked: "[paste their message — remove private details]". 
Write a reply that: answers clearly, stays warm and on-brand, and ends with a helpful next step. 
Tone: [your brand voice]. Under [N] words.
```

**Example — Lola's Boutique (fictional customer "J"):**
```
Act as a friendly rep for a small clothing boutique. 
A customer asked: "Hi do you have the floral dress in a size M and how long is delivery?" 
Write a warm, helpful reply confirming we'll check stock, give a delivery estimate, and invite them to order. Under 80 words.
```

Then you fill in the real stock/delivery info before sending. **The AI writes the wrapper; you supply the facts.** This keeps replies fast *and* accurate.

**Privacy note:** notice the prompt says "remove private details." Before pasting a customer message, strip names, addresses, order numbers, phone/email — replace with placeholders. The AI can craft a perfect reply about "an order" without knowing whose it is.

## Lesson 4.2 — Handling complaints and upset customers

This is where good service is won or lost — and where AI's calm, structured drafting really helps (you're often not calm when a customer is angry).

**The complaint-reply framework (L-E-A-R):**
- **Listen/acknowledge** their frustration.
- **Empathize** sincerely.
- **Act** — state what you'll do to fix it.
- **Reassure** it won't happen again / you value them.

**The prompt:**
```
Act as an experienced customer-service manager. 
A customer is upset because [situation — no private data]. 
Write a reply that acknowledges their frustration, apologizes sincerely, offers [specific fix], and keeps them as a customer. 
Warm, professional, not defensive, no excuses. Under [N] words. Give me 2 versions.
```

**Example — Bright Home Care, a scheduling mix-up:**
```
Act as a home-care service manager. A family is upset because a caregiver arrived 90 minutes late for their mother's visit. 
Write a sincere reply: acknowledge how worrying that was, apologize genuinely, explain the specific steps we're taking so it won't repeat, and offer to make it right. Warm, responsible, no excuses. Under 140 words. 2 versions.
```

**Golden rule:** AI drafts the *tone and structure*; **you** confirm the facts and the fix are real. Never promise something in a reply you can't actually deliver. And for serious complaints (safety, legal, money), a human (often you) reviews before it sends.

## Lesson 4.3 — Your reusable "reply library"

The big win: stop writing the same replies forever. Build a **library** of your top responses as templates.

**How to build it (30 minutes, once):**
1. List your 8–12 most common customer questions/situations.
2. For each, generate a great reply with the prompt above, with [BLANKS] for specifics.
3. Save them in one doc (or a pinned chat / your help-desk tool's canned responses).

**Example library entries:**
- "Hours & location" → `Hi [NAME]! We're open [HOURS] at [LOCATION]. Happy to help — want me to [next step]?`
- "Where's my order" → `Hi [NAME], thanks for your patience! Your order [#] shipped on [DATE] and should arrive by [DATE]. Here's your tracking: [LINK]. Anything else I can help with?`
- "Do you offer X" → `Great question! Yes/No — [answer]. [If yes: how to get it]. Let me know if you'd like to go ahead.`

Now a common message is: find template → fill blanks → send. Seconds, consistent, on-brand. This becomes a key piece of your Module 6 workflow.

## Lesson 4.4 — Privacy and the human handoff

Customer service is the riskiest area for privacy, so be deliberate.

**Never paste into a personal AI tool:**
- Full names tied to health, financial, or sensitive details.
- Addresses, phone numbers, emails, full order/account numbers.
- Payment info of any kind.
- Anything covered by rules in your industry (e.g., health info in home care).

**Do instead:**
- **Anonymize**: "a customer," "[NAME]," "[ORDER#]." The AI drafts just as well.
- **Use templates** so you rarely paste raw messages at all.
- **Use a work-approved tool** if your business handles regulated data — many help-desk and business AI tools offer privacy terms suitable for customer data. Ask before using a personal free account for real customer info.

**When a human MUST handle it (don't fully automate these):**
- Safety issues, medical/legal matters, serious complaints, refunds/money disputes, anything emotional or high-stakes.
- Use AI to *draft* your response, then **you review and send**. For a live chatbot on your site, route these to a person.

AI should make your customer service faster and kinder — never a wall a frustrated person can't get past.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. Before pasting a real customer message into a personal AI tool, you should…**
A) Add more detail
B) Remove/replace personal details with placeholders
C) Translate it
D) Nothing — it's fine
**Answer: B.** Anonymize. The AI drafts just as well without private data.

**Q2. The L-E-A-R complaint framework stands for…**
A) Listen, Email, Argue, Repeat
B) Listen/acknowledge, Empathize, Act, Reassure
C) Learn, Earn, Act, Review
D) Look, Edit, Ask, Reply
**Answer: B.** Acknowledge, empathize, act (the fix), reassure.

**Q3. A "reply library" is…**
A) A list of customer names
B) Saved template replies with blanks for your common messages
C) A new AI tool
D) A refund policy
**Answer: B.** Reusable templates = fast, consistent service.

**Q4. Which situation should a human handle (not full auto)?**
A) "What are your hours?"
B) "Do you have this in blue?"
C) A serious safety complaint or a refund dispute
D) "Where's my tracking number?"
**Answer: C.** High-stakes/emotional/money/safety → human reviews and responds.

**Q5. True or false: It's fine to have AI promise a customer a refund you aren't actually able to give, as long as it sounds nice.**
**Answer: False.** Never promise what you can't deliver. You own what the reply says.
