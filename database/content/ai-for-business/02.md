# Module 2 — Business Writing With AI

**Estimated time:** ~85 minutes · **Lessons:** 5

## Learning objectives
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Draft professional emails fast, in the right tone.
- Produce proposals and quotes that win work.
- Turn messy notes into clean reports and summaries.
- Make the AI write in *your* voice, not a generic robot voice.
- Reuse what you write by turning it into templates.

> Builds on Course 1's R-C-T-F recipe. We'll assume you know it and go straight to applying it to business documents.

---

## Lesson 2.1 — Emails that get results (and take 2 minutes)

Email is probably your #1 time sink. Here's the pattern that handles 90% of business emails.

**The business-email prompt:**
```
Act as a professional [your role]. Write an email to [who] about [situation]. 
Goal: [what you want to happen]. 
Tone: [professional/warm/firm]. Length: under [N] words. 
Include: [key points]. End with a clear next step.
```

**Example — a consultant chasing an overdue invoice (fictional client "Ama Mensah"):**
```
Act as a professional consultant. Write a polite but firm email to a client whose invoice is 2 weeks overdue. 
Goal: get it paid this week without damaging the relationship. 
Tone: warm but firm. Under 120 words. 
Include: the invoice number (#2041), the amount ($1,500), and an easy way to pay. End with a clear next step.
```

You'll get a send-ready draft. Then refine: `make it a touch warmer`, `add that we value their business`, `give me a shorter version for a text reminder`.

**Handling incoming emails too:** paste one you received and say:
```
Here's an email I received: [paste]. Draft a reply that [your goal]. Keep it [tone], under [N] words. Flag anything I should double-check before sending.
```

That "flag anything I should double-check" line is your Course-1 fact-checking habit, built into the workflow.

## Lesson 2.2 — Proposals and quotes that win

A proposal is just a structured, persuasive document — perfect for AI to draft so you can focus on the thinking.

**The proposal prompt:**
```
Act as an experienced [your field] proposal writer. 
Context: [who the client is, their problem, what I offer, my price]. 
Write a proposal with these sections: 1) Their situation, 2) The outcome they want, 3) My approach, 4) What's included, 5) Investment, 6) Next step. 
Tone: confident, clear, no hype. Keep each section short.
```

**Example — Bright Home Care proposing weekend caregiving to a family:**
```
Act as an experienced home-care proposal writer. 
Context: The Owusu family (fictional) needs weekend companionship and light help for their 78-year-old mother. We offer trained caregivers, Saturday & Sunday, 6 hours/day, at $X/hour. 
Write a warm, reassuring proposal with sections: Their situation, The outcome they want, Our approach, What's included, Investment, Next step. 
No hype. Short sections. Reassure them their mum will be safe and respected.
```

Then you add the real numbers, tweak the voice, and send. What used to take an hour takes fifteen minutes.

**Pro move:** keep a "swipe" of a proposal that won. Paste it and say `write the new proposal in this same structure and tone` — instant consistency.

## Lesson 2.3 — Reports and summaries from messy notes

Turning scattered notes into something readable is tedious. AI loves this job.

**Notes → report:**
```
Turn these rough notes into a clear [weekly update / project report / meeting summary] for [audience]. 
Use short sections and bullet points. Pull out: key decisions, action items (with who's responsible), and any risks. 
Keep it under [N] words. Notes: [paste].
```

**Example — consultant after a client call:**
```
Turn these call notes into a client summary email. Sections: What we discussed, Decisions made, Next steps (with owners and dates), Open questions. Professional and concise. Notes: [paste messy notes].
```

**Long document → brief:** attach or paste a long report and ask:
```
Summarize this in one page: the 3 main takeaways, key numbers, and what I should do about it. Note anything I should verify.
```

Remember Course 1's rule: AI is great at *restructuring* your information. Any **numbers or facts it introduces or restates, you verify** before the report goes out.

## Lesson 2.4 — Making AI write in *your* voice

Generic AI writing is the giveaway. Fixing it is easy: **show it your voice.**

**Method 1 — paste samples:**
```
Here are 2 things I've written that sound like me: [paste email 1] [paste post]. 
Learn my tone and style. From now on, write in that voice. First task: [your task].
```

**Method 2 — describe your voice rules:**
```
Write in my style: short sentences, no corporate jargon, warm and direct, occasional question to the reader, no words like "leverage" or "synergy."
```

**Method 3 — build a reusable "voice note"** you paste at the top of any writing prompt:
```
MY VOICE: friendly, plain English, confident not pushy, short paragraphs, I use "you" a lot, I avoid hype words. Keep this voice in everything below.
```

Once AI writes in your voice, business writing stops feeling outsourced and starts feeling like *you, but faster*.

## Lesson 2.5 — Turning writing into reusable templates

The real leverage: don't re-prompt from scratch each time. Save **templates** — prompts with blanks you fill in.

**Template example — "Quote email":**
```
Act as [my role]. Write a quote email to [CLIENT NAME] for [SERVICE]. 
Price: [PRICE]. Timeline: [TIMELINE]. Tone: warm, confident. 
Include what's covered and a clear next step. Under 150 words. Use MY VOICE (above).
```

Keep a simple doc (or a pinned chat) of your 5–10 best templates: quote email, overdue-invoice email, proposal, weekly report, "sorry we missed you" customer reply. Fill the blanks, paste, done. This is the foundation of your Module 6 final project.

## 🛠️ Hands-on exercise (25 min)
1. Take one **real email** you need to write and draft it with the business-email prompt. Refine twice.
2. Create a **voice note** for yourself (Method 3) and re-run the email with it. Compare.
3. Take some **messy notes** and turn them into a clean summary with action items.
4. Turn your email into a **reusable template** with [BLANKS] and save it.
5. Verify any fact/number in your outputs before you'd send them.

**You're done when** you have one send-ready email in your voice and one saved template.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. The best way to stop AI writing from sounding generic is to…**
A) Use longer words
B) Show it samples of your own writing / give it voice rules
C) Write in all caps
D) Use a different tool
**Answer: B.** Show your voice (samples or rules). It matches you.

**Q2. For a notes → report task, AI is best at…**
A) Inventing the numbers for you
B) Restructuring your information into a clean, readable format
C) Deciding your strategy
D) Sending the email automatically
**Answer: B.** It restructures *your* info. You verify facts/numbers.

**Q3. A reusable template is…**
A) A finished document you never change
B) A prompt with [BLANKS] you fill in each time
C) A new AI tool
D) A screenshot
**Answer: B.** Fill-in-the-blanks prompts = speed without starting over.

**Q4. Before sending an AI-drafted invoice email, you should…**
A) Trust every number it wrote
B) Verify the invoice number, amount, and any dates
C) Add more hype
D) Nothing — AI is always right
**Answer: B.** Verify the facts/numbers — the Course 1 habit.

**Q5. True or false: A good AI business email usually ends with a clear next step.**
**Answer: True.** Tell the reader exactly what to do next — it gets results.
