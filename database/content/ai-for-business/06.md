## Lesson 6.1 — From one-off prompts to a prompt system

Across this course you've been saving templates. Now organize them into a **prompt system** — your personal toolkit.

**Set up a simple "AI playbook"** (one doc, or a note, or pinned chats). Structure it like this:
- **My Voice note** (from Module 2)
- **My Audience brief** (from Module 3)
- **Email templates** (quote, follow-up, overdue invoice)
- **Customer reply library** (from Module 4)
- **Research/summary prompts** (from Module 5)
- **Content prompts** (captions, repurpose, plan)

Each entry is a ready-to-use, fill-in-the-blanks prompt. Now your work becomes: *pick the right prompt → fill the blanks → refine → done.* No more staring at a blank chat wondering how to ask.

**Why this matters:** a prompt system is the difference between "I sometimes use AI" and "AI is built into how I work." It's also what makes your workflow trainable — you could hand the playbook to a team member tomorrow.

## Lesson 6.2 — Chaining steps into a workflow

Many business tasks are really **several steps in a row**. You can run them as a smooth chain, each step feeding the next — all in one chat.

**Example — "New client onboarding" (consultant):**
1. `Summarize this intake form into a client profile: [paste].`
2. `Based on that profile, draft a warm welcome email with next steps.`
3. `Now create a simple onboarding checklist for this client as a table.`
4. `Draft 3 questions I should ask them on our kickoff call.`

Four steps, one conversation, ten minutes — and it's consistent every time because the chat carries context from step to step.

**Example — "Weekly content" (creator):**
1. Paste video transcript → `give me a title + description`.
2. → `now 5 Shorts hooks`.
3. → `now 3 captions in my voice`.
4. → `now a newsletter intro`.

**The skill:** map a repeated task into clear steps, then write a prompt for each. Write the chain once; reuse it forever. This is exactly what your final project documents.

## Lesson 6.3 — What "automation" means (and when you need it)

So far, *you* press the buttons. **Automation** means a tool does the repetitive steps for you when something happens — no human clicking required.

**The simple idea:** _"When X happens, automatically do Y."_
- When a **new form is submitted** → automatically **add the person to my email list** and **send a welcome email**.
- When I get an **email with an attachment** → automatically **save the file to a folder**.
- When a **new order comes in** → automatically **send a thank-you message**.

**The tools** (no coding needed — these are "connect your apps" platforms):
| Tool | Free option | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Zapier** | Yes (limited free) | Easiest, huge app library |
| **Make** | Yes (generous free) | Visual, more power for the price |
| **n8n** | Yes (free self-host / cloud trial) | Most control; techier |

These connect your apps (forms, email, spreadsheets, AI) so steps happen on their own. Many now have **AI built in** — e.g., "when an email arrives → ask AI to summarize it → post the summary to my team chat."

> These are third-party tools with their own free and paid plans — **not** included in your Patrik Solutions AI membership. Start on free tiers.

**When do you actually need automation?** Only when a task is (a) repetitive, (b) triggered by a clear event, and (c) you do it often enough that setup pays off. For many small businesses, a great **prompt system + manual chaining** already saves most of the time — automation is the next step up, and the deep version is Course 3. For now, just know what's possible and recognize your best automation candidates.

## Lesson 6.4 — Keeping a human in the loop

Faster is only better if it's still *right*. Every workflow needs a **human checkpoint** — the moment you review before something reaches a customer or goes on record.

Build these in on purpose:
- **Outgoing customer messages** → you (or a trained person) read before send.
- **Anything with facts/numbers** → verify (Course 1 habit).
- **Public content** → a quick honesty + brand check.
- **Money, legal, safety, health** → always a human decides.

In your workflow doc, literally write the line: *"Human check: [who] reviews [what] before [action]."* This is what keeps AI an asset, not a liability — and it's a required part of your final project.

## Lesson 6.5 — 🎓 FINAL PROJECT: Build & document an AI-assisted workflow

Time to turn everything into one real, reusable workflow you'll actually use.

**Your mission:** pick one task you genuinely repeat, build the AI workflow for it, and document it on one page so you (or a teammate) can run it again anytime.

**Good candidates:**
- New client/customer onboarding
- Turning meeting notes into action items + a summary email
- Your weekly content (one idea → a week of posts)
- Handling your most common customer question end-to-end
- Writing and sending your weekly/monthly update or newsletter

**Build it:**
1. **Choose** a repeated task (use one of your "do now" tasks from Module 1).
2. **Map the steps** (Lesson 6.2) — what happens, in order.
3. **Write a reusable prompt** for each step (Lesson 6.1) — with [BLANKS], using your Voice/Audience notes.
4. **Run it** on a real (anonymized) example and refine until the output is genuinely good.
5. **Add the human check** (Lesson 6.4) — where you review before it goes out.
6. **Keep it private-safe** — no real customer/confidential data baked into the prompts.

**Deliver a one-page "Workflow Doc":**
- **Workflow name** and the task it handles
- **Trigger** — what starts it ("every Monday," "a new inquiry arrives")
- **Steps** — numbered, each with its reusable prompt
- **Human check** — who reviews what, before what
- **Time saved** — rough before vs. after
- (Optional) **Automate-later note** — which step(s) a tool like Zapier/Make could run for you someday

**You pass when all five are true** (course rubric): real & repeated · reusable prompt · clear steps · human check named · privacy-safe. That's a workflow you'll keep using — and the exact thing that makes AI part of how your business runs.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. A "prompt system" is…**
A) A new AI model
B) Your organized toolkit of reusable, fill-in-the-blanks prompts
C) A coding language
D) A pricing plan
**Answer: B.** Your saved templates + voice/audience notes, organized to reuse.

**Q2. "Chaining" means…**
A) Locking your chats
B) Running several AI steps in a row, each feeding the next
C) Using many tools at once
D) Copying competitors
**Answer: B.** Step-by-step workflow in one conversation.

**Q3. Automation ("when X → do Y") is worth setting up when the task is…**
A) Rare and random
B) Repetitive, event-triggered, and frequent enough that setup pays off
C) Highly confidential and legal
D) A one-time thing
**Answer: B.** Repetitive + clear trigger + frequent = good automation candidate.

**Q4. Every workflow should include…**
A) At least five AI tools
B) A human checkpoint before things reach customers or the record
C) A paid subscription
D) No review, for speed
**Answer: B.** Human-in-the-loop keeps AI an asset, not a liability.

**Q5. True or false: No-code automation tools like Zapier/Make are included in your Patrik Solutions AI membership.**
**Answer: False.** They're separate third-party tools with their own free/paid plans.
