# Module 3 — Marketing & Social Media With AI

**Estimated time:** ~85 minutes · **Lessons:** 5

## Learning objectives
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Clarify your audience and message so AI gives you *relevant* content.
- Generate captions, posts, and ad copy that sound like you.
- Turn one idea into a week (or month) of content.
- Build a simple, repeatable social media plan.
- Keep marketing honest — no fake claims or made-up reviews.

> Builds on your voice note and templates from Module 2. Marketing is just writing aimed at an audience — you already have the core skills.

---

## Lesson 3.1 — Know your audience and message first

AI will happily write generic marketing. To get content that actually connects, give it three things first:

1. **Who you're talking to** (the specific person, not "everyone").
2. **What they want / worry about** (their real desire or pain).
3. **What you want them to feel or do** (the goal of the post).

**The audience brief** — write it once, reuse it everywhere:
```
MY AUDIENCE: [who they are]. They want [desire]. They worry about [pain]. 
MY BRAND: [what I offer], [my voice: e.g., warm, plain-English, no hype].
Keep all marketing below aimed at this person and this voice.
```

**Example — Bright Home Care:**
```
MY AUDIENCE: adult children (40–60) caring for an aging parent, often from a distance, feeling guilty and stretched. They want their parent safe and happy; they worry about trust and cost.
MY BRAND: compassionate, reliable home care with trained caregivers. Voice: warm, reassuring, plain English, no hype.
```

Paste that above any marketing prompt and your content instantly stops being generic.

## Lesson 3.2 — Captions, posts, and ad copy

With your audience brief in place, generating content is fast.

**Social caption:**
```
[AUDIENCE BRIEF above]
Write 3 Instagram caption options announcing [thing]. Warm and specific to my audience. 
Each under 60 words, with a gentle call to action. Suggest 5 relevant hashtags.
```

**A short value post (educational):**
```
Write a helpful LinkedIn post for my audience about [topic]. Teach one useful thing, keep it skimmable with short lines, end with a question to spark comments. No hype.
```

**Simple ad copy:**
```
Write 3 short Facebook ad variations for [offer]. Each: a hook line, 2 lines of benefit, one clear call to action. Honest and specific — no exaggerated claims.
```

**Example — Lola's Boutique new arrival:**
```
[audience: women 25–40 who love affordable, stylish pieces and shop on their phones]
Write 3 Instagram captions for a new floral dress ($45, limited stock). Fun, friendly, under 50 words each, with a CTA to "tap to shop" and 5 hashtags.
```

Then refine like always: `make option 2 punchier`, `give me a version for a Story`, `add urgency honestly (limited stock, not fake scarcity)`.

## Lesson 3.3 — One idea → a week of content (repurposing)

The secret to never running out of posts: take one piece and spin it into many.

**The repurpose prompt:**
```
Here's one piece of content: [paste a blog post, video transcript, or a few paragraphs]. 
Turn it into: 
- 3 short social captions (different angles) 
- 1 "listicle" post (5 quick tips) 
- 3 ideas for short-form video hooks 
- 1 email to my list 
Keep my voice and audience. Honest, no invented facts.
```

**Example — content creator with one long video:**
```
Here's my video transcript: [paste]. Turn it into: a YouTube title + description, 5 Shorts hooks (first line only), 3 Instagram captions, and 1 newsletter intro. Keep my casual, direct voice.
```

This is how solo business owners and creators post consistently without living in their phone. (It's exactly the repurposing idea behind tools like ReelsDey — here you're doing it by hand with prompts.)

## Lesson 3.4 — A simple content plan you'll actually follow

A plan beats motivation. Keep it dead simple.

**The content-plan prompt:**
```
[AUDIENCE BRIEF]
Create a simple 4-week content plan, 3 posts/week. 
Use a rotation of post types: 1 educational, 1 behind-the-scenes/story, 1 promotional. 
Give me the theme and a one-line idea for each post, in a table (Week, Day, Type, Idea). 
Keep ideas specific to my audience.
```

You'll get a grid you can work from for a month. Each week, take that week's three ideas and run them through your caption prompts. Plan once, execute fast.

**The 3-type rotation** (why it works): educational builds trust, story builds connection, promotional drives action. Rotating them keeps you from being "all ads" (which people tune out) or "never selling" (which earns nothing).

## Lesson 3.5 — Marketing honestly (this protects your brand)

AI makes it easy to produce lots of marketing — which makes honesty *more* important, not less.

**Rules:**
- **No invented facts or stats.** If AI writes "trusted by 10,000 families," delete it unless it's true. Made-up numbers destroy trust and can be illegal.
- **No fake reviews or testimonials.** Never generate a customer quote and present it as real. (Also against platform rules and often the law.)
- **No exaggerated promises.** "Guaranteed results," "get rich," "cures X" — avoid. Be specific and truthful instead.
- **Disclose AI where required.** Some platforms and ad rules require labeling AI-generated or AI-altered media. Check the platform's current policy.
- **You're responsible.** You publish it; you own every claim in it.

Honest marketing powered by AI = more content, same integrity. That's the brand that lasts.

## 🎬 Instructor demonstration script
1. **Write the audience brief live** for a sample business. Then generate 3 captions *without* it and 3 *with* it. "Same prompt, night-and-day relevance."
2. **Repurpose one piece:** paste a short paragraph and fan it into captions + a listicle + video hooks. "One idea just became a week of posts."
3. **Generate a 4-week plan table.** Show how you'd execute week 1 by feeding ideas into the caption prompt.
4. **Catch a false claim:** deliberately prompt for an ad, and if it adds "trusted by thousands," stop and delete it. "This is where you protect your brand — never ship a claim you can't back."
5. Recap the 3-type rotation.

## 🛠️ Hands-on exercise (25 min)
1. Write your **audience brief** and save it.
2. Generate **3 captions** for something real you'd post. Pick and refine one.
3. **Repurpose** one existing piece (a post, a paragraph, a transcript) into at least 4 content pieces.
4. Generate a **4-week content plan** table.
5. Scan everything for **honesty**: delete any claim, stat, or testimonial you can't back up.

**You're done when** you have a saved audience brief, one polished caption, and a 4-week plan.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. The first thing to give AI before generating marketing is…**
A) Your logo
B) Your audience and message (who it's for, what they want)
C) A budget
D) Ten hashtags
**Answer: B.** Audience + message = relevant content instead of generic.

**Q2. "Repurposing" means…**
A) Reposting the same thing
B) Turning one piece of content into many formats/angles
C) Deleting old posts
D) Buying ads
**Answer: B.** One idea → many posts. The key to consistency.

**Q3. The educational/story/promotional rotation prevents…**
A) Running out of hashtags
B) Being all-ads (tuned out) or never-selling (no revenue)
C) Posting too little
D) Spelling mistakes
**Answer: B.** Balance builds trust *and* drives action.

**Q4. AI wrote "trusted by 10,000 happy families" and it's not true. You should…**
A) Keep it — it sounds good
B) Delete it; never publish claims you can't back up
C) Change it to 20,000
D) Add a fake review to match
**Answer: B.** Invented claims and fake reviews break trust (and often the law).

**Q5. True or false: You're responsible for the accuracy of AI-generated marketing you publish.**
**Answer: True.** You publish it, you own every claim.
