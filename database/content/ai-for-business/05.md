# Module 5 — Research & Organizing Business Information

**Estimated time:** ~80 minutes · **Lessons:** 4

## Learning objectives
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Use AI to research topics faster — and verify what it tells you.
- Summarize long documents, reports, and transcripts into usable briefs.
- Turn messy information into clean tables, lists, and comparisons.
- Build simple knowledge docs (FAQs, SOPs) your business can reuse.

> This module uses Course 1's fact-checking habit the most — research is exactly where hallucinations bite. We'll make verification part of the flow.

---

## Lesson 5.1 — Researching faster (without getting burned)

AI is a brilliant *starting point* for research — it orients you fast — but it is **not a search engine with guaranteed facts**. Use it to understand and structure, then verify specifics.

**The research prompt:**
```
I need to understand [topic] for [purpose]. 
Give me: a plain-English overview, the 5 key things I should know, common mistakes, and 5 questions I should investigate further. 
Flag anything that may be outdated or that I must verify independently.
```

**Example — a consultant prepping for a new industry (retail logistics):**
```
I'm a consultant new to retail inventory management. Give me a plain-English overview, the 5 core concepts, the common mistakes small shops make, and 5 questions I should research further. Flag anything that could be out of date.
```

**The critical habit — two layers of verification:**
1. **Live facts** (prices, laws, current stats, recent events): if your tool has **web browsing** turned on, ask it to search and **give links you can click**. If it doesn't, treat its facts as "to be confirmed" and check them yourself.
2. **Important specifics**: numbers, names, quotes, legal/financial/health claims → verify with the actual source. Ask: `What should I verify here, and where would I check it?`

Think of AI research as a smart briefing from a sharp assistant who sometimes misremembers. Fantastic for speed; never your only source for facts that matter.

## Lesson 5.2 — Summarizing long documents and transcripts

Drowning in long PDFs, reports, and call recordings? This is one of AI's most valuable business uses.

**Document → brief:**
```
Summarize this [document type] for [who/purpose]. Give me: 
- The 3 main points 
- Any key numbers or dates 
- What it means for me / what I should do 
- Anything I should double-check 
Keep it under one page. Document: [attach or paste].
```

**Long transcript → notes:**
```
Here's a meeting/call transcript: [paste]. Give me: decisions made, action items (with owner + due date), open questions, and a 3-sentence summary.
```

**Compare multiple documents:**
```
Here are two proposals from vendors: [paste/attach both]. Compare them in a table: price, timeline, what's included, pros, cons. Then tell me which fits a [small budget / fast timeline] best and why.
```

**Example — retail shop comparing suppliers:** paste two supplier quotes and get a clean comparison table in seconds. You still confirm the real numbers, but the *analysis and structure* are done.

> Privacy reminder: if a document contains customer or confidential data, use a work-approved tool or redact sensitive parts first.

## Lesson 5.3 — Turning mess into structure (tables, lists, comparisons)

AI is exceptional at **reorganizing** information you already have. This is low-risk (your facts) and huge time savings.

**Mess → table:**
```
Here's a jumble of notes/info: [paste]. Organize it into a clean table with columns: [name the columns you want].
```
**Make a comparison:**
```
Compare [options] on [criteria] in a table. Then give a one-line recommendation for [my situation].
```
**Prioritize a list:**
```
Here are 15 tasks/ideas: [paste]. Group them into themes, then rank by [impact/effort] and suggest what to do first.
```
**Extract structured data:**
```
From this text, pull out every [date / name / price / action item] into a list: [paste].
```

**Example — content creator with 30 messy video ideas:** paste them, get them grouped into themes and ranked by likely interest. From chaos to a content plan in one prompt.

Because you supplied the information, there's little hallucination risk here — AI is just tidying your own material.

## Lesson 5.4 — Building reusable knowledge docs (FAQs, SOPs)

Turn one-time research into permanent business assets.

**Make an FAQ:**
```
Based on these common customer questions and my answers: [paste], write a clean, friendly FAQ document grouped by theme. Keep my voice.
```
**Write an SOP (standard operating procedure — a step-by-step how-to for a task):**
```
Turn this process into a clear SOP someone new could follow: [describe or paste the steps]. 
Format: goal, who does it, step-by-step numbered instructions, what "done" looks like, common mistakes to avoid.
```
**Example — Bright Home Care onboarding SOP:** describe how you onboard a new caregiver, and AI formats it into a proper SOP you can hand to a manager. Now it's documented, repeatable, and trainable — not stuck in your head.

These knowledge docs are gold: they make your business less dependent on *you* remembering everything, and they feed directly into the automations in Module 6.

## 🎬 Instructor demonstration script
1. **Research a topic** with the Lesson 5.1 prompt. When it gives facts, ask `which of these should I verify and where?` — model the two-layer habit. If browsing is on, show it returning clickable links.
2. **Summarize a long doc:** attach a multi-page PDF, get a one-page brief with "what to double-check." 
3. **Compare two things in a table** (two vendor quotes). Point out how fast the structure appears — then confirm the numbers.
4. **Mess → structure:** paste 15 scrambled ideas, get them themed and ranked.
5. **Build an SOP** from a described process. "You just turned knowledge in your head into a trainable document."

## 🛠️ Hands-on exercise (25 min)
1. **Research** a topic relevant to your work; then ask AI which facts to verify and verify one.

3. **Reorganize** a messy list of yours into a table or ranked list.

**You're done when** you've verified at least one researched fact and produced one reusable knowledge doc.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. AI research is best treated as…**
A) A guaranteed-accurate encyclopedia
B) A fast, smart starting point whose facts you verify
C) A replacement for all thinking
D) Useless
**Answer: B.** Great for speed and structure; verify the facts that matter.

**Q2. For current facts (prices, laws, recent events), the safest approach is…**
A) Trust the AI's memory
B) Use web browsing for clickable sources, or verify independently
C) Guess
D) Ask twice
**Answer: B.** Live facts need live sources or your own verification.

**Q3. Which task has the *lowest* hallucination risk?**
A) Asking for recent statistics from memory
B) Reorganizing information you supplied into a table
C) Asking for academic citations
D) Asking for a current law
**Answer: B.** Tidying *your own* info is low-risk; AI isn't inventing facts.

**Q4. An SOP is…**
A) A sales offer
B) A step-by-step document for doing a task consistently
C) A social post
D) A spreadsheet formula
**Answer: B.** Standard Operating Procedure — repeatable how-to.

**Q5. True or false: Summarizing a confidential document in a personal free AI tool with the client's name included is fine.**
**Answer: False.** Redact/anonymize or use a work-approved tool for confidential data.
