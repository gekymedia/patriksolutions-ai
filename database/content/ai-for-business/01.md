# Module 1 — Finding Your AI Opportunities

**Estimated time:** ~70 minutes · **Lessons:** 4 · _Lesson 1.1 is a free preview._

## Learning objectives
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Identify the tasks in your work where AI saves the most time.

- Build your own personal "AI task list" to work through in this course.

## Lesson 1.1 — Where AI actually earns its keep _(preview)_

Most people sprinkle AI randomly and give up when it doesn't change their week. The winners do the opposite: they find the **two or three tasks** where AI saves the most time, and nail those first.

The best AI opportunities share four traits — remember them as **R-R-L-L**:

- **Repetitive** — you do it again and again (weekly reports, the same customer questions).
- **wRitten/language-based** — it involves text, drafting, or reshaping words (AI's home turf, from Course 1).
- **Low-stakes to draft** — a wrong first draft is cheap to fix (vs. a legal filing where errors are costly).
- **Low-privacy** — it doesn't require pasting sensitive customer or company secrets.

A task that's all four is a goldmine. Examples across different businesses:
- **Boutique owner (Lola's Boutique):** writing product descriptions for new arrivals. Repetitive ✔ Written ✔ Low-stakes ✔ Low-privacy ✔ → perfect.
- **Consultant:** turning messy call notes into a clean client summary. ✔✔✔✔
- **Home-care agency (Bright Home Care):** drafting the first version of recruitment posts for caregivers. ✔✔✔✔
- **Retail shop:** replying to the same "do you have this in stock / what are your hours" messages. ✔✔✔ (watch privacy if order details are involved).
- **Content creator:** turning one long video into captions, a title, and a description. ✔✔✔✔

**Where AI does NOT belong (yet):** final legal/medical/financial decisions, anything needing guaranteed-correct numbers you can't verify, and anything requiring real confidential data in a personal tool. Use AI to *prepare* those, not to *decide* them.

The goal of this module: find *your* R-R-L-L tasks so the rest of the course pays off immediately.

## Lesson 1.2 — The "time audit" that finds hidden hours

You can't optimize what you can't see. Let's find where your time actually goes.

**Do this (10 minutes):** list every recurring task you did last week that involved writing, reading, replying, summarizing, planning, or organizing. Don't filter yet — just dump them. Typical finds:
- Replying to emails and messages
- Writing posts/captions
- Making quotes or proposals
- Answering the same customer questions
- Writing or updating documents
- Summarizing notes, calls, or articles
- Planning your week / to-do lists
- Creating reports

**Then estimate** two numbers for each: how many times per week (frequency) and roughly how long each takes (minutes). Multiply them → **weekly minutes**. The big numbers are where AI can give you hours back.

You don't need to be precise. The point is to *see* the pattern. Most people are shocked how much time goes into repetitive writing — exactly what AI is best at.

## Lesson 1.3 — Scoring and ranking your opportunities

Now rank the list so you attack the best ones first. Score each task 1–3 on four things:

| Factor | Score 1 | Score 2 | Score 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time saved/week** | A little | Some | A lot |
| **How repetitive** | Rare | Sometimes | Constant |
| **AI fit** (language-based) | Weak | Partial | Strong |
| **Privacy-safe** | Risky | Needs care | Safe |

Add the four scores. Anything **10–12** = do it now. **7–9** = good, do next. **Below 7** = skip or wait.

**Worked example — Bright Home Care (fictional):**
- Caregiver recruitment posts: time 3, repetitive 2, fit 3, privacy 3 = **11 → do now.**
- Replying to family inquiries: time 3, repetitive 3, fit 3, privacy **1** (contains personal care details) = 10, but the privacy 1 is a flag → do it *with anonymizing/templates*, not raw.
- Monthly compliance report: time 2, repetitive 2, fit 2, privacy 1 = 7 → later, and only for the narrative parts, never the regulated data.

This scoring stops you from wasting effort and keeps you out of privacy trouble.

## Lesson 1.4 — Three traps to avoid

Before you start, dodge the classic mistakes:

1. **The "AI does my whole job" trap.** AI drafts and assists; you still review, decide, and own the result. Expect a 70–90% draft you polish, not a finished product.
2. **The privacy trap.** The fastest way to get in trouble is pasting a customer's or employee's private details into a personal AI account. We'll use **templates with placeholders** so you get the speed without the risk (next modules).
3. **The shiny-tool trap.** You don't need 15 AI tools. One good assistant + good prompts handles 90% of business writing. Master that before chasing the latest app.

## 🎬 Instructor demonstration script
1. **Do a live 2-minute time audit** on screen for a sample business (pick home care or a boutique). List 8 recurring tasks, add rough weekly-minute estimates. "Look — three tasks eat half the week, and they're all writing."
2. **Score the top three** using the 1–3 table out loud. Show one scoring high (recruitment posts) and one flagged for privacy (family inquiries).
3. **Show the payoff fast:** take the #1 task and get AI to do a first draft with a quick R-C-T-F prompt. "That just saved 20 minutes — and we'll make it a reusable template by Module 6."
4. Recap R-R-L-L and the "do now / do next / skip" cutoffs.

## 🛠️ Hands-on exercise (20 min)
1. Do the **time audit** (Lesson 1.2): list your recurring written/organizing tasks + weekly minutes.
2. **Score** your top 5 with the 1–3 table (Lesson 1.3).
3. Circle your **top two "do now"** tasks — these become your working examples for the rest of Course 2.
4. For each, write one sentence on the **privacy angle**: does it involve real customer/company data? If yes, note "use placeholders."

**You're done when** you have a ranked list and two chosen "do now" tasks with privacy noted.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. The four traits of a great AI opportunity (R-R-L-L) are…**
A) Rich, Rapid, Large, Lucky
B) Repetitive, wRitten/language-based, Low-stakes to draft, Low-privacy
C) Risky, Rare, Legal, Long
D) Routine, Remote, Local, Live
**Answer: B.** Repetitive, written, low-stakes, low-privacy = a goldmine task.

**Q2. A task scores high on everything but "privacy" (score 1). You should…**
A) Do it anyway with real data
B) Skip AI forever
C) Do it using placeholders/templates so no private data is exposed
D) Only do it on weekends
**Answer: C.** Keep the speed, remove the risk with anonymizing/templates.

**Q3. What realistic output should you expect from AI on a business task?**
A) A finished product needing zero review
B) A 70–90% draft you polish and own
C) Nothing useful
D) A guaranteed-correct legal document
**Answer: B.** Draft + your polish. You review and own it.

**Q4. The "shiny-tool trap" is…**
A) Using one assistant well
B) Chasing many AI tools instead of mastering prompting on one
C) Buying a shiny laptop
D) Using templates
**Answer: B.** One good assistant + good prompts beats tool-hopping.

**Q5. True or false: The best way to start is to pick your 2–3 highest-scoring tasks, not to AI-ify everything at once.**
**Answer: True.** Focus beats sprinkling. Win the top tasks first.
