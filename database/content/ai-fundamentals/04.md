# Module 4 — Writing Clear Prompts

**Estimated time:** ~60 minutes · **Lessons:** 5

> A **prompt** is just the message you type to the AI. Learning to write good ones is the single skill that changes AI from "meh" to "wow." This is the most important module in the course.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Use a simple 4-part recipe to write clear prompts.
- Give the AI a role, context, a task, and a format.
- Steer tone, length, and audience on purpose.
- Fix a weak answer with follow-ups instead of giving up.

---

## Lesson 4.1 — Why prompts matter (garbage in, garbage out)

The AI can't read your mind. It only has the words you give it. Watch the difference:

- ❌ `write a post` → you'll get a generic, bland post about nothing in particular.
- ✅ `Write a short Instagram caption for my home-care business announcing weekend visits. Warm and reassuring tone, aimed at adult children caring for elderly parents. End with a gentle call to message us.` → you'll get something you could almost post as-is.

Same AI. Totally different result. The second prompt isn't longer for the sake of it — it tells the AI **who it's for, what it's about, how it should sound, and what to include.** That's the whole game.

**Mindset:** think of the AI as a brilliant new assistant on their first day. They're capable, but they don't know your business, your audience, or your preferences. The clearer your instructions, the better their work. You're not "programming" — you're briefing a smart helper.

---

## Lesson 4.2 — The 4-part prompt recipe (R-C-T-F)

Here's a recipe you can use for almost anything. You don't always need all four, but when an answer disappoints, it's usually because one is missing.

**R — Role:** who the AI should act as.
> `Act as an experienced customer-service manager.`

**C — Context:** the background it needs.
> `I run a small online clothing shop. A customer's order arrived damaged and they're upset.`

**T — Task:** exactly what you want it to do.
> `Write a reply apologizing, offering a free replacement, and keeping them as a happy customer.`

**F — Format:** how the answer should look.
> `Keep it under 120 words, friendly but professional, and give me 2 versions.`

**Put together:**
```
Act as an experienced customer-service manager. I run a small online clothing shop. A customer's order arrived damaged and they're upset. Write a reply that apologizes, offers a free replacement, and keeps them as a happy customer. Keep it under 120 words, friendly but professional, and give me 2 versions.
```

Compare that to just `reply to an angry customer`. Night and day. **Memorize R-C-T-F** — it's your reliable default for the rest of your life with AI.

## Lesson 4.3 — Steering tone, length, and audience

Three dials you can turn on any prompt:

**Tone** — just say it: `friendly`, `professional`, `warm and reassuring`, `confident but not pushy`, `funny`, `formal`. You can even say `in the tone of a kind teacher`.

**Length** — be specific: `in 3 sentences`, `about 100 words`, `one short paragraph`, `a bulleted list of 5 points`, `a single text message`.

**Audience** — tell it who will read this: `explain to a 10-year-old`, `for busy executives`, `for someone who has never used a computer`, `for Instagram`. The AI adjusts vocabulary and examples automatically.

Example combining all three:
```
Explain how compound interest works, in a warm and simple tone, in about 120 words, for a teenager saving their first money.
```

Change one dial and re-run to see how much control you have:
```
Now explain the same thing for a small-business owner, in a confident professional tone, as 4 bullet points.
```

## Lesson 4.4 — Show, don't just tell (examples & "do this / not that")

Two power-moves that instantly improve results:

**1. Give an example of what you want.** If you have a style you like, paste it:
```
Here's an email I wrote that I'm proud of: [paste it]. Write a new email about [new topic] in that same style and tone.
```
The AI will match your voice far better than any description could.

**2. Tell it what to avoid.** Negative instructions work:
```
Write a product description for a water bottle. Keep it simple and honest. Do NOT use hype words like "revolutionary," "game-changer," or "amazing."
```

**3. Ask for structure you can use.** Request tables, numbered steps, or sections:
```
Give me a 5-day beginner workout plan as a table with columns: Day, Focus, Exercises, Time.
```

These three moves — example, constraints, structure — turn a good prompt into a great one.

## Lesson 4.5 — When the answer isn't right: fix it, don't quit

Beginners get a so-so answer and give up. Pros refine. Keep these follow-ups handy:

- `Make it shorter / longer.`
- `Make it warmer / more formal / simpler.`
- `That's too generic — add specific details about [X].`
- `Give me 3 different versions so I can choose.`
- `Keep the second half, rewrite the first half.`
- `You misunderstood — what I actually meant was [clarify].`
- `What questions do you need me to answer to make this better?` ← surprisingly powerful; the AI will interview *you*.

Also a pro tip: **let the AI improve your prompt.**
```
I want to ask you to [goal]. Before you answer, write me the best possible prompt I could give you for this, then answer it.
```

Refining is normal and expected. The first answer is a starting point, not a verdict.

---

## 🎬 Instructor demonstration script

1. **Show the contrast.** Type `write a post`. Then type the full R-C-T-F version from Lesson 4.1. Put them side by side. "Same AI. One sentence of effort. Completely different result."
2. **Build R-C-T-F live** for a real task the audience suggests (e.g., a reminder email). Add each part and watch the answer sharpen.
3. **Turn the dials.** Take one answer and run `shorter`, then `more formal`, then `for a 10-year-old`. Show how much control they have.
4. **Show-don't-tell:** paste a sample of writing and say `match this style`. Point out the voice change.
5. **The self-improving prompt:** run `write me the best prompt for [goal], then answer it`. "Let the AI do the hard part of prompting for you."
6. **Fix a bad answer** with two follow-ups to model persistence over quitting.

## ✍️ Copy-and-paste practice prompts
The R-C-T-F template to reuse forever:
```
Act as [ROLE].
Context: [who I am, the situation, any details].
Task: [exactly what I want you to do].
Format: [length, tone, audience, structure, number of versions].
```
Dial-turners:
```
Rewrite that in a warmer tone and about half as long.
Explain it again for someone who has never done this before.
Give me 3 versions with different tones so I can pick one.
```
Self-improving prompt:
```
I want help with [goal]. First write the best prompt I could give you for this, then answer it.
```

---

## 🛠️ Hands-on exercise (20 min)
1. Pick a real task you'd actually do (an email, a caption, an explanation, a plan).
2. First ask it the **lazy way** (3–4 words). Save the answer.
3. Now write it with the full **R-C-T-F** recipe. Save that answer.
4. Compare them. Note what improved.
5. Turn **two dials** (tone and length) with follow-ups.
6. Finally, run the **self-improving prompt** on the same goal and see if it beats your own.

**You're done when** your R-C-T-F version is clearly better than your lazy version, and you've refined it with at least two follow-ups.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. What are the four parts of the R-C-T-F prompt recipe?**
A) Read, Count, Try, Finish
B) Role, Context, Task, Format
C) Request, Confirm, Test, Fix
D) Review, Copy, Tone, Feedback
**Answer: B.** Role, Context, Task, Format.

**Q2. Your caption came out generic. The most likely fix is…**
A) Switch AI tools
B) Give more context and specifics about your audience and goal
C) Type it in all caps
D) Ask the same thing again unchanged
**Answer: B.** Generic output usually means missing context/specifics.

**Q3. Which prompt will best match your personal writing voice?**
A) "Write like a professional"
B) "Here's an email I like: [paste]. Write the new one in this style."
C) "Be creative"
D) "Use big words"
**Answer: B.** Showing an example beats describing a style.

**Q4. The follow-up "What questions do you need me to answer to make this better?" is useful because…**
A) It ends the chat
B) The AI interviews you to get the missing details
C) It translates the text
D) It's required
**Answer: B.** It flips it around so the AI gathers the context it's missing.

**Q5. True or false: A disappointing first answer means AI can't do the task.**
**Answer: False.** The first answer is a starting point. Refining with follow-ups is the normal path to a great result.
