## Lesson 5.1 — 12 everyday uses (that save real time)

You now have the skills — here's where to point them. Real tasks, with a starter prompt for each:

1. **Tricky messages** — `Help me reply to this text politely but firmly: [paste].`
2. **Emails** — `Write a professional email asking my landlord to fix the heating. Firm but polite, under 100 words.`
3. **Meal planning on a budget** — `Give me a 5-day dinner plan for 2 people, under $60, with a shopping list. We don't eat pork.`
4. **Understanding documents** — attach a letter/bill/form: `Explain what this is asking me to do and any deadline.`
5. **Studying & learning** — `Explain [topic] simply, then quiz me with 5 questions and check my answers.`
6. **Planning & lists** — `Make me a packing list for a 4-day work trip to a cold city.`
7. **Writing anything** — a toast, a bio, a cover letter, a complaint, a thank-you note.
8. **Translating & simplifying** — `Translate this into Spanish, then give a simpler English version too.`
9. **Comparing options** — `I'm choosing between these two phones: [specs]. Make a pros/cons table for a casual user.`
10. **Brainstorming** — `Give me 15 name ideas for a home-cleaning business, friendly and easy to remember.`
11. **Summarizing** — paste a long article or transcript: `Summarize in 5 bullets and tell me the main takeaway.`
12. **Getting unstuck** — `I need to [goal] but I don't know where to start. Give me the first 3 steps.`

The habit to build: when a task feels slow, fiddly, or blank-page-scary, ask yourself *"could I get a first draft from AI?"* Usually, yes.

## Lesson 5.2 — The fact-checking habit (your safety net)

From Module 2 you know AI can hallucinate. Here's the simple routine to make checking automatic — it takes seconds and saves embarrassment.

**The CARE check** — run it on anything you'll send, publish, or act on:
- **C — Claims:** Are there facts, numbers, dates, names, or quotes? Those are the risky bits.
- **A — Authority:** For anything important, confirm with a trusted source (official site, a quick search, the actual document).
- **R — Recency:** Could this be out of date? If it depends on current info, verify it's current.
- **E — Expert:** Does being wrong hurt health, money, safety, or legal standing? If yes, a qualified human confirms before you act.

Two quick tricks that make checking easier:
```
List every fact in your last answer that I should independently verify, and what I'd search to check each.
```
```
Where in your last answer are you least confident, and why?
```

**Rule of thumb:** lean fully on AI for *language and ideas*; always verify the *facts*. That single habit is the difference between AI making you look sharp and AI making you look careless.

## Lesson 5.3 — Privacy: what never to paste into an AI

This is important and simple. When you type something into a third-party AI tool, it leaves your device and goes to that company's servers. On many tools, your chats **may be used to improve the model** unless you turn that off. So:

**Never paste into a public AI tool:**
- Passwords, PINs, or security answers.
- Full credit-card, bank-account, or Social-Security/national-ID numbers.
- Other people's private data (a client's medical details, an employee's salary, a customer's full record) — especially at work, where rules may apply.
- Confidential company secrets or anything under an NDA.
- Anything you'd be upset to see leaked.

**Safer habits:**
- **Turn off training on your data.** In the tool's **Settings → Data controls (or Privacy)**, switch off "use my chats to train the model" / "improve the model for everyone." (Names vary; the option is there on the major tools.)
- **Anonymize.** Swap real names/numbers for placeholders: "Client A," "$X," "[address]." The AI can still help with the *shape* of the task.
- **Use work-approved tools for work data.** Many employers provide a business/enterprise version with stronger privacy. Ask before pasting work info into a personal free account.
- **Remember it's not a vault.** Treat an AI chat like a message to a helpful stranger's company — useful, but not the place for secrets.

## Lesson 5.4 — Using AI responsibly and honestly

A short, grown-up code of conduct so you use this well:

- **You are responsible for what you send.** AI drafts; you decide and own the final result. "The AI said so" is never an excuse.
- **Be honest about AI's role where it matters.** For schoolwork, follow your school's rules — using AI to *learn and draft* is often fine; passing off AI work as entirely your own may not be. When in doubt, ask and disclose.
- **Don't use it to deceive or harm** — no impersonating real people, no fake reviews, no misinformation, no generating content to trick or scam others.
- **Keep your own skills sharp.** Use AI to go faster and learn more, not to stop thinking. Read the output, understand it, improve it. That's how you grow instead of getting dependent.
- **Respect others' privacy and work.** Don't paste people's private info; don't pass off others' work as your own just because AI reshuffled it.

Used this way, AI is a genuine superpower that makes you more capable without compromising who you are.

## Lesson 5.5 — 🎓 FINAL PROJECT: "AI helps with one real thing this week"

Time to put it all together on something genuinely useful to you.

**Your mission:** choose one real task from your actual life or work this week, and use AI to help you complete it — applying everything you learned.

**Pick a real task.** Examples (choose your own):
- Write a difficult email or message you've been avoiding.
- Make a 5-day meal plan on a budget with a shopping list.
- Summarize a long document, article, or set of notes you need to understand.
- Plan a trip, an event, or a weekend.
- Write a cover letter, bio, or complaint letter.
- Study something: get it explained simply and quiz yourself.

**Do it with your new skills:**
1. **Set up** your AI assistant (Module 3).
2. **Write an R-C-T-F prompt** — role, context, task, format (Module 4).
3. **Refine** with at least one follow-up to improve the answer.
4. **CARE-check** at least one fact or detail (Module 5) — verify it.
5. **Make it yours** — edit the result so it truly fits you.
6. **Protect privacy** — don't paste anything sensitive; anonymize if needed.

**Submit a short write-up (half a page is plenty):**
- The task you chose and why it's real.
- The exact prompt you used.
- What the AI gave you (paste or screenshot).
- What you verified (which fact, and how).
- What you changed to make it yours.

**You pass when all four are true** (see the course rubric):
1. Real task ✔ 2. Clear R-C-T-F prompt ✔ 3. You checked at least one fact ✔ 4. You improved the result ✔

That's it — you'll have used AI end-to-end on something that actually mattered. That's the whole point.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. Which should you NEVER paste into a public AI tool?**
A) A rough draft of a blog post
B) Your bank account number and PIN
C) A question about cooking
D) A list of city names
**Answer: B.** Passwords, PINs, full financial/ID numbers, and others' private data stay out.

**Q2. In the CARE check, the "E" reminds you to…**
A) Email the AI company
B) Get a qualified human/expert when being wrong could hurt health, money, safety, or legal standing
C) Export the chat
D) Use emojis
**Answer: B.** Expert = loop in a professional for high-stakes decisions.

**Q3. A good way to use AI with sensitive info is to…**
A) Paste it all and hope for the best
B) Anonymize it — replace real names/numbers with placeholders
C) Only use it at night
D) Translate it first
**Answer: B.** Anonymizing lets AI help with the task's shape without exposing real data.

**Q4. Who is responsible for a final document you created with AI's help?**
A) The AI company
B) No one
C) You
D) Your internet provider
**Answer: C.** You own and are responsible for what you send or publish.

**Q5. True or false: It's fine to turn off "use my chats to train the model" in your AI tool's settings.**
**Answer: True.** It's your choice and a good privacy habit, especially for anything you'd rather keep private.
