**Estimated time:** ~120 minutes · **Lessons:** 5 (includes the guided capstone)

## Lesson 8.1 — Scoping: pick the right problem

The most common reason AI projects fail is **bad scope** — too big, too vague, or solving a problem no one has. Scope well:

- **Start from a real, painful, repeated problem.** "We lose time triaging inquiries," not "let's do something with AI."
- **Make it small and specific.** One task, one audience, one clear output. You can expand later.
- **Check it's a good AI fit** (Course 1/Course 2 R-R-L-L: repetitive, language-based, low-stakes to draft, privacy-manageable).
- **Define success in one sentence.** "Cut inquiry triage from 20 min/day to 5, with no missed urgent cases."

**Good first AI solutions** (pick one for your capstone):
- A custom **FAQ/policy assistant** grounded in your docs (RAG).
- An **inquiry triage + first-draft reply** automation.
- A **content repurposing** assistant (one input → a week of posts).
- A **meeting-notes → summary + action items** workflow.
- A **proposal/quote drafting** assistant in your voice.

Narrow and real beats broad and vague — every time.

## Lesson 8.2 — The design doc (one page, before you build)

Spend 30 minutes planning to save hours building. Your design doc:

```
SOLUTION NAME: 
PROBLEM: what hurts today (and how much time/money it costs)
USERS: who will use it
SUCCESS METRIC: how we'll know it worked (one measurable sentence)
HOW IT WORKS: trigger → steps → output (the workflow/assistant design)
KNOWLEDGE/DATA: what documents or data it needs
GUARDRAILS: privacy rules, human checkpoints, what it must refuse
TOOLS: which AI tool / automation platform / tiers
COST ESTIMATE: setup time + ongoing (subscriptions, API/tokens) — Lesson 8.4
RISKS: top 2 things that could go wrong + mitigation
```

This doc is also your capstone's first deliverable. It forces the decisions that matter before you sink time into building the wrong thing.

## Lesson 8.3 — Build it (no-code), using everything you've learned

Now assemble the pieces from Modules 1–7:

1. **Prompts** → write them as reliable templates with variables and strict output (Module 1).
2. **Chain** → if multi-step, design clean hand-offs, branches, checkpoints (Module 2).
3. **Package** → build it as a custom assistant with system instructions and guardrails (Module 3), or an automation (Module 5).
4. **Ground** → add your documents as knowledge if it needs your facts (Module 4, RAG).
5. **Wire** → connect trigger → AI → destination if automating (Module 5).
6. **Secure & govern** → add the anti-injection rule, least privilege, human checkpoints, privacy rules (Module 7).

**Build iteratively:** get the simplest version working end-to-end first (even clunky), then improve. Don't polish step 1 for an hour before you know step 3 works.

> **⌨️ Optional coding:** if your solution needs custom logic, a special integration, or high-volume/low-cost processing, build the AI calls in a small script (Module 5 sidebar) with the same prompts, guardrails, secret keys, and spending caps. Not required — a no-code assistant/automation is a complete capstone.

## Lesson 8.4 — Costing: time and money (so there are no surprises)

AI solutions have two cost types. Estimate both honestly:

**Setup cost (one-time):** your hours to design, build, test (Modules 6). Be realistic — a solid assistant might be 3–8 hours the first time.

**Running cost (ongoing):**
- **Subscriptions:** any paid AI tool tier or automation platform plan (third-party, not included in your membership). List them.
- **API/token usage** (if you use an API): cost scales with volume. Rough method: estimate *requests per month × average tokens per request × the provider's price per token*. Providers publish current prices — **check them** (they change). Start with a **spending cap** and watch real usage for a week, then project.
- **Maintenance time:** updating knowledge docs, fixing prompts, reviewing outputs.

**Then weigh it against the value:** time saved × your hourly value, errors avoided, revenue enabled. A $20/month tool that saves 5 hours/week is an easy yes. An expensive build that saves 10 minutes a month is not. **Positive, honest ROI is the goal** — don't build AI for its own sake.

> Avoid overcommitting: start on free/cheap tiers, prove value at small scale, then scale spend as the value is confirmed.

## Lesson 8.5 — Pilot, launch, measure, maintain

Don't go from "it works on my desk" to "everyone depends on it." Roll out in stages:

1. **Pilot (small, real).** Use it yourself or with one trusted person on real work for a week. Keep the old way as backup. Run your eval (Module 6).
2. **Fix what the pilot reveals.** Tighten prompts, add guardrails, update docs.
3. **Launch (to your real audience — even if that's just you/your team).** Add any needed disclosure (Module 7). Write a 3-line "how to use it" so others can.
4. **Measure against your success metric.** Did triage drop from 20→5 min? Are outputs good? Track it.
5. **Maintain.** Schedule a periodic check: review outputs, update knowledge, watch costs, handle any incidents. An AI solution is a garden, not a statue — it needs tending as your business and the tools change.

A launch isn't the finish line; it's the start of a loop: use → measure → improve.

## 🎓 CAPSTONE PROJECT — Build & launch a useful AI solution

Bring the whole course together. Build something real you'll actually use.

**Steps:**
1. **Scope** a real problem (Lesson 8.1) and write your **one-page design doc** (8.2).
2. **Build** the solution — a custom assistant and/or an automation — using Modules 1–5 (no-code is fully fine; coding optional).
3. **Ground** it in your documents if needed (Module 4).
4. **Govern** it: anti-injection rule, least privilege, privacy rules, a human checkpoint (Module 7).
5. **Test** it with a small eval (Module 6): ~10 inputs incl. edge + should-refuse; score; fix; re-run.
6. **Cost** it (Lesson 8.4): setup time + ongoing subscriptions/tokens.
7. **Pilot & "launch"** it (8.5) — use it on real work; add disclosure if needed; write a 3-line usage note.

**Deliverables (submit these):**
- ✅ One-page **design doc**
- ✅ The **working solution** (link/screenshots of the assistant and/or automation)
- ✅ Your **test results** (the eval spreadsheet or a summary: what passed, what failed, what you fixed)
- ✅ One documented **limitation + safeguard**
- ✅ A short **governance note** (privacy + the human-oversight point)
- ✅ A rough **running cost** estimate

**You pass when all six rubric criteria are met:** solves a real problem · it actually works · you tested it · you found its limits · it's governed · it's costed.

That's it — you'll have designed, built, tested, governed, costed, and launched a working AI solution end-to-end. That's not a course exercise; that's the real job.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. The most common reason AI projects fail is…**
A) The model is too smart
B) Bad scope — too big, too vague, or solving a non-problem
C) Not enough tools
D) Too much testing
**Answer: B.** Narrow, real, measurable scope is the fix.

**Q2. A design doc should be written…**
A) After launch
B) Before building, to force the key decisions
C) Never
D) Only if coding
**Answer: B.** Plan on one page before you build.

**Q3. Running cost of an API-based solution scales mainly with…**
A) The weather
B) Usage (requests × tokens × price) — so set a spending cap and watch volume
C) The number of logins
D) Nothing
**Answer: B.** Pay-per-use by tokens; cap and monitor.

**Q4. Before a full launch you should…**
A) Skip testing to save time
B) Pilot on real work (with a backup), run an eval, fix issues, then launch and measure
C) Auto-send everything immediately
D) Delete the old process first
**Answer: B.** Pilot → fix → launch → measure → maintain.

**Q5. True or false: A good AI solution has a clear, measurable success metric and a positive, honest return on the time/money it costs.**
**Answer: True.** Build for real value, not for AI's sake.
