# Module 4 — Connecting AI to Your Documents (an intro to RAG)

**Estimated time:** ~95 minutes · **Lessons:** 4

## Learning objectives
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Explain, in plain words, what RAG is and why it matters.
- Give an AI assistant your own documents so it answers from *your* knowledge.
- Do this with no-code tools (and know the coding path exists).
- Keep document-based AI accurate and safe (citations, limits, privacy).

**No-code core.** One **⌨️ Optional coding** sidebar on building RAG yourself.

---

## Lesson 4.1 — The problem RAG solves

By default, an AI model only knows what it learned during training — not *your* business. It doesn't know your prices, your policies, your product manuals, your past projects. Ask it about your return policy and it will **guess** (and maybe hallucinate).

**RAG** stands for **Retrieval-Augmented Generation**. Strip the jargon and it means: *before the AI answers, go retrieve the relevant bits from your documents and give them to the AI to base its answer on.*

**Plain analogy:** imagine a brilliant assistant answering customer questions. Without RAG, they answer from memory (sometimes wrong). With RAG, every time a question comes in, they first **flip to the right page of your company binder**, read it, and answer **from that page**. Same assistant — but now grounded in your actual, current information.

**Why this is a big deal:**
- Answers come from **your** facts, not the model's guesses.
- You can **update the documents** and the answers update — no retraining.
- The AI can **cite where it got the answer**, so you can verify.
- It dramatically **reduces hallucination** for anything covered by your docs.

RAG is how you build an assistant that actually knows *your* world — an FAQ bot, a policy helper, a "search our manuals" assistant.

---

## Lesson 4.2 — How RAG works (the simple version)

Three steps happen behind the scenes:

1. **Store your documents** in a searchable form. (Tools break your files into chunks and index them so they can be searched by *meaning*, not just keywords.)
2. **Retrieve** the chunks most relevant to the user's question.
3. **Generate** an answer using those chunks as the source, ideally citing them.

You don't have to run these steps yourself — no-code tools do all three when you "upload knowledge." But understanding them helps you get better results:
- **Good source documents = good answers.** Clean, well-organized files (clear headings, up-to-date, no contradictions) retrieve far better than messy ones.
- **Chunking matters.** Very long, unstructured docs retrieve worse. Break big manuals into clear sections.
- **Retrieval can miss.** If the answer isn't phrased near the question's words/meaning, it might not get retrieved. Good structure and clear wording in your docs help.

> One term you'll hear: **embeddings** — a way of turning text into numbers that capture meaning, so "What's your refund window?" can match a document section titled "Returns policy" even without shared keywords. No-code tools handle embeddings for you.

## Lesson 4.3 — Do it NO-CODE (three practical ways)

You can give AI your documents today, without writing code:

**Option A — Upload knowledge to a custom assistant (from Module 3).**
In ChatGPT GPTs, Claude Projects, or Gemini Gems, upload your files (FAQ, price list, policies, SOPs) as the assistant's **knowledge**. Now it answers from them. Add this rule to the system instructions:
```
Answer using ONLY the uploaded knowledge files. If the answer isn't in them, say "I don't have that in my documents" and suggest who to ask. When possible, cite the document/section you used.
```

**Option B — Attach documents per chat.**
For one-off needs, just attach the PDF/doc in a normal chat and ask questions about it ("What does section 4 say about cancellations?"). Simple, free, no setup — but you re-attach each time.

**Option C — A dedicated document-Q&A tool.**
Tools like **Google NotebookLM** (free) let you load a set of documents and ask questions, with citations back to the source. Great for research and "ask my files" use cases. (Third-party tool; free tier; not included in your membership.)

**Choosing:** one-off → attach in chat (B). A reusable assistant your team uses → custom assistant with knowledge (A). Researching a document set with citations → NotebookLM (C).

**Keep knowledge fresh:** out-of-date documents produce confidently wrong answers. Put a "last updated" date on key files and review them on a schedule.

---

## Lesson 4.4 — Accuracy, citations & privacy for document AI

Grounding AI in your docs reduces hallucination — but doesn't eliminate it. Stay disciplined:

- **Force "only from the documents."** Without that instruction, the model may blend in its own guesses. Make it say "not in my documents" when it's not.
- **Demand citations.** "Cite the document and section for each answer." Then you (or the user) can verify in seconds.
- **Spot-check.** Periodically ask known questions and confirm the answers match your real docs.
- **Watch contradictions.** If two documents disagree, the AI may pick wrongly. Resolve conflicts in your source files.
- **Privacy & access:** uploading documents means they go to the tool's servers. Don't load confidential/regulated data into a personal free account. For sensitive material, use a business/enterprise tier with appropriate privacy terms, and control **who can access** the assistant (a customer-facing bot should only have customer-safe docs — never internal pricing margins, staff data, or secrets).

**The payoff done right:** an assistant that answers your customers and team accurately, from your current, approved information, with receipts. That's one of the highest-value AI builds for any business — and your capstone could be exactly this.

> **⌨️ Optional coding sidebar — building RAG yourself**
> Developers build RAG with: a way to chunk documents, an **embeddings** model to turn chunks into vectors, a **vector database** (e.g., Pinecone, Chroma, Supabase/pgvector) to store and search them, and code that retrieves the top chunks and passes them to the model with the question. Frameworks like LlamaIndex or LangChain wire this together. You get more control (custom chunking, filters, scale) but it's real engineering. **Not required** — the no-code options above deliver real RAG for most business needs.

## 🎬 Instructor demonstration script
1. **Show the problem:** ask a plain chat "What's [your business]'s refund policy?" — watch it guess. "It doesn't know you."
2. **No-code RAG (Option A or C):** upload a short FAQ/policy file to a custom assistant or NotebookLM. Ask the same question — now it answers from the file and cites it.
3. **Force the guardrail:** ask something *not* in the docs; show it saying "not in my documents" (because the instruction told it to). "That one rule prevents a lot of hallucination."
4. **Update a doc** and re-ask to show answers change with the source — "no retraining needed."
5. **Privacy point:** note what you'd never upload to a personal account and how you'd restrict a customer-facing bot's knowledge.

## 🛠️ Hands-on exercise (35 min)
1. Gather 1–3 real (non-sensitive) business documents (FAQ, policy, price ranges, an SOP).
2. **Give them to an AI** via a custom assistant's knowledge, or load them in NotebookLM.
3. Add/confirm the **"only from the documents + cite sources"** rule.
4. Ask **5 questions**: 3 the docs answer (check the citations) and 2 they *don't* (confirm it says "not in my documents").
5. Note one **gap** (a common question your docs don't cover) to fix later.

**You're done when** your assistant answers correctly from your docs *with citations* and correctly refuses what's not covered.

## ✅ Quiz (with answers & explanations)

**Q1. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) means…**
A) The AI retrains on your data overnight
B) Relevant bits from your documents are retrieved and given to the AI to base its answer on
C) A faster model
D) A pricing plan
**Answer: B.** Retrieve from your docs → ground the answer in them.

**Q2. The main benefit of RAG for a business is…**
A) Prettier answers
B) Answers come from your own, current facts (fewer hallucinations) and update when docs change
C) It removes the need for prompts
D) It's free forever
**Answer: B.** Grounded, current, verifiable answers without retraining.

**Q3. A free no-code way to ask questions across a set of documents with citations is…**
A) A vector database
B) Google NotebookLM (or uploading knowledge to a custom assistant)
C) Writing Python
D) Email
**Answer: B.** No-code document-Q&A with sources.

**Q4. To reduce hallucination in a document assistant, you should instruct it to…**
A) Be creative
B) Answer only from the documents and say "not in my documents" otherwise, with citations
C) Guess confidently
D) Ignore the files
**Answer: B.** Ground + refuse-when-absent + cite.

**Q5. True or false: Uploading documents to an AI tool means they leave your device, so you shouldn't upload confidential/regulated data to a personal free account.**
**Answer: True.** Use a business/enterprise tier with proper privacy for sensitive material, and restrict who/what can access it.
