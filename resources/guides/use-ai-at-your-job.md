The people who get ahead with AI at work are not the ones who hand everything over to a machine — they are the ones who use it to produce a fast first draft, then apply their own expertise to finish the job. Used well, AI clears the repetitive work so you can focus on thinking, judgement, and the parts only you can do.

## Everyday tasks AI handles well

Here is where AI saves the most time in a normal workday:

- **Email** — draft replies, soften or sharpen tone, and summarise long threads.
- **Documents** — summarise reports, contracts, and meeting notes into key points.
- **Reports** — turn rough notes and figures into a clean first draft.
- **Brainstorming** — generate ideas for projects, campaigns, names, and solutions.
- **Spreadsheets** — write formulas, clean up messy data, and explain what a sheet is doing.
- **Planning** — break a project into tasks, timelines, and checklists.
- **Communication** — prepare talking points, agendas, and presentation outlines.

Think of AI as a capable assistant who works fast but needs checking — not a replacement for your knowledge of the business.

## How to prompt for work tasks

The quality of what you get back depends on how you ask. A strong work prompt usually includes:

1. **Role** — "Act as an experienced project manager."
2. **Task** — "Summarise this report into five key points for senior managers."
3. **Context** — paste the relevant details or background.
4. **Format** — "Use bullet points and keep it under 150 words."

**Weak prompt:** "Write an email about the delay."
**Strong prompt:** "Draft a polite, professional email to a client explaining that delivery is delayed by three days, apologising briefly, and offering a new date. Keep it under 120 words."

Keep a personal file of prompts that work well for your role so you can reuse them.

## Doing it responsibly

This is the part that protects your job and your reputation, so take it seriously.

- **Follow your employer's policy.** Many organisations have rules on which AI tools are approved. Check before you use anything, and use only sanctioned tools.
- **Protect sensitive data.** Do not paste confidential information, customer records, financial data, passwords, or anything personal into a public AI tool. When in doubt, leave it out or remove identifying details first.
- **Always verify.** AI can sound confident and still be wrong. Check facts, figures, names, dates, and any legal or financial detail before anything leaves your hands.
- **Own the output.** Once you send it, it is your work. Read every word as if you wrote it, because as far as your employer is concerned, you did.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use approved tools | Paste confidential or personal data |
| Draft, then review and edit | Send raw AI output unchecked |
| Verify facts and numbers | Trust AI on legal, medical, or financial claims |
| Follow company policy | Hide that you used AI if disclosure is expected |

## How AI skills help you grow at work

Becoming the person on your team who uses AI well is a genuine career advantage.

- You deliver faster and free up time for higher-value work.
- You can take on bigger projects without being overwhelmed.
- You become the go-to person others ask for help, which raises your profile.
- You build a future-proof skill as more roles start to expect AI fluency.

### Tips to get started
1. **Start with one task** you do often, like email or summaries.
2. **Track the time you save** so you can show the value.
3. **Share useful prompts** with your team to build goodwill.
4. **Keep learning** — small improvements in how you prompt add up quickly.
