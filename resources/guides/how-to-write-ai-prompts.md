The difference between a disappointing AI answer and a genuinely useful one is almost always the prompt. A good prompt gives the AI everything a smart new assistant would need to do the job well: who to be, what to do, and what "good" looks like.

## The Simple 5-Part Prompt Framework

Think of every strong prompt as five pieces. You don't always need all five, but reaching for them turns a vague question into a clear instruction.

1. **Role** — tell the AI who to be. *"Act as an experienced customer-service writer."*
2. **Task** — say exactly what you want done. *"Write a reply to this complaint."*
3. **Context** — give the background. The customer, the product, what happened, who's reading.
4. **Format** — say how the answer should look. A list, a table, 3 paragraphs, under 100 words.
5. **Tone** — set the voice. Friendly, formal, plain, confident, warm.

A quick way to remember it: **Role, Task, Context, Format, Tone.**

## Before and After

**Before (vague):**

```
Write me a product description for my shop.
```

**After (specific):**

```
Act as a copywriter for a small online fashion shop in Accra.
Write a product description for a handmade kente-print tote bag.
Buyers are young professionals who care about local craft and quality.
Format: one short paragraph plus 3 bullet points of features.
Tone: warm, confident, no hype.
```

The second prompt will give you something you can almost paste straight onto your page. The first will give you something generic you have to rewrite.

## How to Iterate on the Answer

Your first result is a draft, not the final word. Treat the AI like a colleague and give feedback:

- *"Good start. Make it shorter and warmer."*
- *"Rewrite the second point so it mentions the free delivery."*
- *"Give me three more options with different opening lines."*

Each follow-up builds on what came before, so you rarely need to start over. Keep refining until it sounds like you.

## Reusable Prompt Patterns

Once a prompt works, save it and reuse it with small changes. A few patterns worth keeping:

| Pattern | Example starter |
|---|---|
| **Explain simply** | "Explain [topic] to a complete beginner in plain English, with one everyday example." |
| **Draft then improve** | "Draft a [thing]. Then list 3 ways it could be stronger." |
| **Summarise** | "Summarise this text in 5 bullet points a busy person can read in 30 seconds." |
| **Compare** | "Compare these options in a table with pros and cons." |
| **Role-play practice** | "Act as a tough client. Interview me for this job so I can practise." |

Keep a simple document of your best prompts. Over time this becomes your personal toolkit.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Being too vague.** "Make it better" means little. Say what "better" is.
- **Asking for everything at once.** Break big jobs into steps.
- **Giving no context.** The AI can't read your mind or see your business.
- **Accepting the first draft.** The real quality comes from one or two rounds of feedback.
- **Forgetting to check facts.** AI can sound confident and still be wrong, so verify anything important.

## A Quick Practice Exercise

Pick one real task from your week — an email, a caption, a plan. Write it first as a one-line question, then rewrite it using all five parts of the framework. Run both. The gap you see is exactly the skill you're building.
